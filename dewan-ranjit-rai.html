Lieutenant Colonel Dewan Ranjit Rai

Maha Vir Chakra (Posthumous)

1st Battalion, The Sikh Regiment · 6 February 1913 – 27 October 1947

On the morning of 27 October 1947, as Jammu and Kashmir faced a grave military crisis, the first Indian Army troops were airlifted to Srinagar. They were commanded by Lieutenant Colonel Dewan Ranjit Rai of the 1st Battalion, The Sikh Regiment.

The raiders advancing towards Srinagar threatened the capital and the airfield through which Indian reinforcements would arrive. Rai led his men towards the danger. During the operations that followed, he was killed in action. His initiative and determined leadership helped check the raiders’ advance, buying valuable time for the defence of Srinagar.

01 — Before the uniform

Dewan Ranjit Rai was born on 6 February 1913 in Gujranwala, then part of British India and now in Pakistan. He attended Bishop Cotton School in Shimla and was among the early cadets associated with the Indian Military Academy at Dehradun, established in 1932.

His maternal uncle was General P. N. Thapar, who would later become Chief of the Indian Army. The surviving accounts consulted for this story reveal only part of Rai’s personal life. His military record, however, preserves the responsibility he carried as commanding officer of 1 Sikh during one of the most consequential moments in the early history of independent India.

02 — The call to Kashmir

In October 1947, tribal raiders entered Jammu and Kashmir from the north-west and advanced towards Srinagar. Baramula lay along their route to the capital, while Srinagar’s airfield was vital to bringing Indian troops into the region.

Following the acceptance of Jammu and Kashmir’s accession to India, plans were made to airlift troops to Srinagar. The battalion selected for the first air movement was 1 Sikh, commanded by Rai. In The Story of the Integration of the Indian States, V. P. Menon recounts that the battalion was stationed near Gurgaon when it received the assignment. Rai’s tasks were to secure the airfield, assist the local government in maintaining law and order, and, if possible, drive away raiders who had entered the city.

The mission allowed little time for preparation. On the morning of 27 October 1947, Rai and his men landed at Srinagar. Their arrival marked the beginning of the Indian Army’s airlift into Kashmir.

03 — The advance towards Baramula

After landing, Rai learned that the raiders had reached Baramula, a strategically important point on the road towards Srinagar. He recognised the danger of allowing their advance to continue unchecked and decided to advance towards Baramula.

The enemy was well armed, possessing machine guns and mortars, and had a numerical advantage. As the fighting developed, Rai assessed the situation and decided to withdraw towards Pattan along the Baramula–Srinagar road. It was during these operations that Rai was killed in action on 27 October 1947, according to the Times of India account published on 29 October 2013.

The official Maha Vir Chakra citation records that Rai personally conducted reconnaissance and operations at considerable personal risk. It credits his disregard for personal danger and his determined, inspired leadership with helping stop the raiders sufficiently far from Srinagar to enable Indian forces to build up for their eventual decisive defeat.

For his gallantry, Lieutenant Colonel Dewan Ranjit Rai was posthumously awarded the Maha Vir Chakra, India’s second-highest wartime gallantry award.

04 — The person behind the uniform

A Times of India report published in 2013, drawing on recollections from Rai’s family, described a family tradition of military service spanning generations. His granddaughter, Priya Shergill, remembered him as an accomplished dancer and recalled his courtesy and respect towards women.

These recollections add a personal dimension to a life more commonly remembered through the circumstances of his death and the honour bestowed upon him. They remind us that the men remembered in military history were individuals with interests, relationships and lives beyond the battlefield.

05 — What his actions made possible

The defence of Srinagar in October 1947 depended on time: time to bring in reinforcements, organise positions and prevent the raiders from reaching the capital unchecked. Rai’s decision to oppose the raiders beyond Srinagar carried considerable risk. The official citation recognises that his leadership helped hold them at a distance that allowed Indian forces to build up for the fighting that followed.

His death did not end the struggle for Kashmir. But his actions became part of the effort to defend Srinagar during a decisive opening phase of the conflict. Remembering Rai means recognising the judgement required of a commander when information is incomplete, the responsibility of leading others into danger, and the personal cost that military service can demand.

Veer Smriti remembers Lieutenant Colonel Dewan Ranjit Rai for his courage, his leadership, and the life he gave in the defence of Srinagar.
